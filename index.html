CASE BRIEFING

HOW THE GAME WORKS

The game has two halves of equal size. Levels 1 to 3 cover homicide: malice aforethought and the felony-murder rule, voluntary manslaughter, and classifying homicides by degree. Levels 4 to 6 cover causation: cause-in-fact, intervening acts, and a proximate-cause boss fight.

  • Levels 1, 2, 5 and the Boss are shooters: answer targets descend toward your ship, and you shoot the right one before they land.
  • Level 3 is a ladder climb: climb to the right homicide grade and lock it in.
  • Level 4 is a catcher game: steer the paddle so each falling fact card lands in the right bin.
  • No clock runs until you press "I've read this: begin." Each card or wave has its own clock after that.
  • A correct answer scores 100 points plus up to 50 for speed (Boss: 150 plus up to 50). A wrong answer or a timeout costs a life.
  • Every 5 correct in a row earns an extra life, and so does a flawless level. Lives bank up to 6.
  • After every answer you get an explanation and a Next button, so read at your own pace.

LEVEL 1: MALICE INVADERS

THE MISSION

Murder is the unlawful killing of another human being with malice aforethought. Five invaders descend in formation, one for each answer. Shoot the one that names the state of mind the facts show. If there's no malice at all, shoot E. Some cases test the felony-murder rule itself and carry their own targets.

CONTROLS: ← / → move your ship, Space fires. With a mouse, click a target and your ship flies under it and fires. Shortcut: press the target's letter (A, B...) to auto-aim and fire.

READ THE TARGETS FIRST

  1. Intent to kill. Express malice.
  2. Intent to inflict great bodily injury. Implied malice.
  3. Abandoned and malignant heart. Reckless indifference to an unjustifiably high risk to human life. Implied malice.
  4. Felony murder. Intent to commit a felony. Implied malice.
  5. No malice. None of the four states of mind is present.

DEADLY WEAPON RULE

Intentional use of a deadly weapon authorizes a permissive inference of intent to kill. A deadly weapon is any instrument (or, in some limited circumstances, any part of the body) used in a manner calculated or likely to produce death or serious bodily injury.

FELONY-MURDER LIMITS

The prosecution must prove the underlying felony was committed or attempted. The felony must be independent of the killing. Under the majority rule, death must have been a foreseeable result of the felony.

LEVEL 2: HEAT SEEKERS

THE MISSION

Voluntary manslaughter is a killing that would otherwise be murder but is distinguishable by adequate provocation, i.e., a killing in the heat of passion. Here you play defense counsel. Shoot MITIGATED if all four common-law tests are met. If not, shoot the test that fails.

CONTROLS: Same as Level 1. ←/→ + Space, click a target, or press its letter.

THE FOUR TESTS

(a) The provocation would arouse sudden and intense passion in an ordinary person, causing loss of self-control. (b) D was in fact provoked. (c) There was not sufficient time for a reasonable person's passions to cool. (d) D in fact did not cool off.

WHEN PROVOCATION IS ADEQUATE

Most frequently recognized: being subjected to a serious battery or a threat of deadly force, and discovering one's spouse in bed with another person.

MERE WORDS

At common law, "mere words" were inadequate as a matter of law. Modern courts are more likely to let the jury decide whether mere words or similar matters are adequate provocation.

IMPERFECT SELF-DEFENSE

Some states reduce murder to manslaughter even when D was at fault in starting the altercation, or unreasonably but honestly believed deadly force was necessary.

READ THE TARGETS FIRST

  1. MITIGATED: all four tests met, so voluntary manslaughter.
  2. INADEQUATE PROVOCATION: wouldn't arouse sudden, intense passion in an ordinary person (test a).
  3. TIME TO COOL: enough time passed for a reasonable person to cool (test c).
  4. NOT IN FACT: D wasn't in fact provoked, or in fact cooled off (tests b and d).

The two imperfect self-defense cases have their own targets.

LEVEL 3: LADDER OF DEATH

THE MISSION

Your detective starts at the bottom of the homicide ladder. Climb to the most appropriate classification and lock it in before time runs out. Assume a statute that divides murder into degrees the way the outline describes.

CONTROLS: ↑ / ↓ climb, Space or Enter locks in. With a mouse, click a rung to climb there and lock in.

THE LADDER

First degree: all murders are second degree unless the prosecution proves a deliberate and premeditated killing ("deliberate" = decided in a cool and dispassionate manner; "premeditated" = actually reflected on the idea of killing, if only briefly), a killing during an enumerated felony, or a killing by lying in wait, poison, or torture.

Second degree: any other murder, including a killing during a felony that isn't on the list, where a statute covers it.

Voluntary manslaughter: a killing in the heat of passion after adequate provocation.

Involuntary manslaughter: criminal negligence (MPC: recklessness), or an unlawful act, e.g., a malum in se misdemeanor, or a malum prohibitum one where death was foreseeable.

Not a criminal homicide: no criminal liability for the death, e.g., a justifiable or excusable homicide, or no "human being" killed under the born-alive rule.

LEVEL 4: BUT-FOR BLASTER

THE MISSION

Now the causation half. Fact cards drop from the sky. Slide the yellow paddle under the correct bin before the card lands. The only question is cause-in-fact. Proximate cause comes in Level 5, so a card can be a cause-in-fact even if proximate cause would fail. If the card lands with the paddle in the middle, it falls between the bins and counts as a miss.

CONTROLS: ← / → move the paddle. ↓ or Space slams the card down now for a bigger speed bonus. With a mouse, click the left or right bin to move there and slam.

◀ LEFT BIN: ACTUAL CAUSE

The result would not have occurred "but for" the defendant's conduct.

RIGHT BIN ▶: NOT AN ACTUAL CAUSE

The result would have occurred anyway, without the defendant's conduct.

HASTENING AND SIMULTANEOUS ACTS

An act that hastens an inevitable result is still a cause of it. Simultaneous acts by two or more persons may each be independently sufficient causes of a single result.

PREEXISTING CONDITION

A preexisting condition that makes the victim more susceptible to death doesn't break the chain; the defendant "takes the victim as he finds him."

LEVEL 5: CHAIN GUN

THE MISSION

Every case has an intervening act between the defendant's conduct and the death. Each case is two waves of targets. In wave 1, shoot the kind of intervening act: ACT OF NATURE, ACT OF A THIRD PARTY, or ACT OF THE VICTIM. In wave 2, shoot CHAIN HOLDS (D is still the proximate cause) or CHAIN BREAKS (the intervening act shields D).

CONTROLS: Same as Level 1. ←/→ + Space, click a target, or press its letter.

THE GENERAL RULE

An intervening act shields the defendant from liability if the act is a mere coincidence or is outside the foreseeable sphere of risk created by the defendant's act. The chain is broken only by a "superseding factor."

THE OUTLINE'S EXAMPLES

Act of nature: lightning striking the victim is a mere coincidence. Third party: negligent medical care is a foreseeable risk, but gross negligence or intentional mistreatment is not. Victim: refusing treatment or suicide from unbearable pain may be found foreseeable.

BOSS: THE LEGAL CAUSE MOTHERSHIP

THE MISSION

The Mothership hovers at the top of the screen, launching four answer targets at a time. Shoot the right one to knock down its shields. Nine correct hits destroy it. This round covers the rules of proximate cause, including how causation works in felony murder. Correct hits are worth more, and a miss still costs a life.

PROXIMATE CAUSE

Problems arise only when death occurs because of the defendant's acts but in a manner not intended or anticipated. A defendant is responsible for all "natural and probable" consequences of his conduct, even if he didn't anticipate the precise manner. Only a superseding factor breaks the chain.

YEAR AND A DAY

At common law, the victim had to die within one year and one day of the injury, or there could be no homicide prosecution. Most states that have reviewed the rule have abolished it.

AGENCY VS. PROXIMATE CAUSE

Agency theory: the killing must be committed by the felon or his agent. Proximate-cause theory: felons are liable for innocent victims killed by someone other than a co-felon, since they set the events in motion.

DEATH OF A CO-FELON

The majority Redline view: liability for murder cannot be based on the death of a co-felon resulting from resistance by the victim or police pursuit.
