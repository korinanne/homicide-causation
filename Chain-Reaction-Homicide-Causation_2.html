HOW THE GAME WORKS

The game has two halves of equal size. Levels 1 to 3 cover homicide: the forms of malice and the felony-murder rule, heat-of-passion mitigation, and grading. Levels 4 to 6 cover causation: actual cause, intervening causes, and a proximate-cause boss fight.

  • Levels 1, 2, 5 and the Boss are shooters: answer targets descend toward your ship, and you shoot the right one before they land.
  • Level 3 is a ladder climb: climb to the right homicide grade and lock it in.
  • Level 4 is a catcher game: steer the paddle so each falling fact card lands in the right bin.
  • No clock runs until you press "I've read this: begin." Each card or wave has its own clock after that.
  • A correct answer scores 100 points plus up to 50 for speed (Boss: 150 plus up to 50). A wrong answer or a timeout costs a life.
  • Every 5 correct in a row earns an extra life, and so does a flawless level. Lives bank up to 6.
  • After every answer you get an explanation and a Next button, so read at your own pace.

LEVEL 1: MALICE INVADERS

THE MISSION

Common-law murder is an unlawful killing of another human being with malice aforethought. Five invaders descend in formation, one for each answer. Shoot the one that names the form of malice the facts show. If there's no malice at all, shoot E. A few cases test the felony-murder rule itself and carry their own targets.

CONTROLS: Same as Level 2. ←/→ + Space, click a target, or press its letter.

READ THE TARGETS FIRST

  1. Intent to kill. An inference of intent to kill may be made if a deadly weapon was used intentionally.
  2. Serious bodily harm. The defendant intended serious bodily injury, not death, and the victim died.
  3. Depraved heart. An unintentional killing from reckless indifference to an unjustifiably high risk to human life.
  4. Felony murder. An unintended killing during an inherently dangerous felony (BARRK).
  5. Below malice. An unintentional killing that is negligent or careless, not murder.

FELONY-MURDER RULE BASICS

The felony must be inherently dangerous (BARRK: burglary, arson, rape, robbery, kidnapping) and independent of the killing. Under merger, an assault resulting in death is not felony murder, and aggravated battery can't be the predicate. The slides note the controversy: it imposes murder punishment without intent to kill, and it's defended as discouraging felony conduct.

LEVEL 2: HEAT SEEKERS

THE MISSION

The slides distinguish two manslaughters: recklessly causing death, which the prosecutor charges, and heat of passion, a mitigation of murder that the defendant raises. Here you play defense counsel. For each killing, shoot MITIGATED if heat of passion works. If it fails, shoot the element that fails.

PROVOCATION

The defendant was actually provoked by the victim, and a reasonable person would have been provoked. A serious battery, a threat of deadly force, or discovering a spouse's adultery qualifies. Words alone cannot be provocation (People v. Pouncey), though the slides ask about "informational words."

NO COOLING-DOWN PERIOD

The defendant did not have enough time to cool down, and a reasonable person would not have cooled down.

LOSS OF SELF-CONTROL

The defendant actually lost control, and a reasonable person would have. The provocation must cause the killing: no mitigation when the intent to kill was formed before the provocation.

EXTREME EMOTIONAL DISTURBANCE

State v. Elliott: broader than "heat of passion," with no cooling-down inquiry.

READ THE TARGETS FIRST

  1. MITIGATED: every element is met, so it's voluntary manslaughter.
  2. NO PROVOCATION: no adequate provocation.
  3. COOLED OFF: enough time passed for a reasonable person to cool off.
  4. NOT IN PASSION: the defendant wasn't actually inflamed, or formed the intent to kill first.

One case uses the extreme-emotional-disturbance test and has its own targets.

LEVEL 3: LADDER OF DEATH

THE MISSION

Your detective starts at the bottom of the homicide ladder. Climb to the most appropriate charge and lock it in before time runs out. Assume a statute defines first-degree murder as a willful, deliberate, and premeditated killing, or a killing during a BARRK felony.

CONTROLS: ↑ / ↓ climb, Space or Enter locks in. With a mouse, click a rung to climb there and lock in.

TRADITIONAL GRADING (FROM THE SLIDES)

First degree: malice aforethought, premeditated, deliberate. Under the statute in State v. Thompson, premeditation means the intent or knowledge precedes the killing by any length of time to permit reflection, but not the instant effect of a sudden quarrel or heat of passion.

Second degree: malice aforethought only, including depraved heart.

Voluntary manslaughter: passion killing.

Involuntary manslaughter: gross negligence (criminal negligence); under the MPC, recklessness. Also a killing during a malum in se misdemeanor.

Not criminal homicide: no living human being was killed by the defendant.

LEVEL 4: BUT-FOR BLASTER

THE MISSION

Fact cards drop from the sky. Slide the yellow paddle under the correct bin before the card lands. The only question is actual cause (cause-in-fact). Proximate cause comes in Level 5, so a card can be an actual cause even if it would fail the fairness test later. If the card lands with the paddle in the middle, it falls between the bins and counts as a miss.

CONTROLS: ← / → move the paddle. ↓ or Space slams the card down now for a bigger speed bonus. With a mouse, click the left or right bin to move there and slam.

◀ LEFT BIN: ACTUAL CAUSE

But for the defendant's act, the victim would not have died. Setting in motion the forces that kill counts, and so does an act that accelerates death.

RIGHT BIN ▶: NOT AN ACTUAL CAUSE

The death would have happened the same way without the act, or an independent cause killed the victim before the defendant's act could.

SUBSTANTIAL FACTOR

With multiple causes, the defendant's act must be a substantial factor. Simultaneous independent acts can each be an actual cause even if the victim would have died from either one.

PREEXISTING CONDITION

A victim's preexisting condition that contributes to the death doesn't supplant the defendant's conduct as an actual cause.

LEVEL 5: CHAIN GUN

THE MISSION

Every case has an intervening cause: an actual cause that occurs after the defendant's voluntary act. Each case is two waves of targets. In wave 1, shoot DEPENDENT or INDEPENDENT. In wave 2, shoot CHAIN HOLDS (D is the proximate cause) or CHAIN BREAKS (the intervening cause relieves D).

CONTROLS: ← / → move your ship, Space fires. With a mouse, click a target and your ship flies under it and fires. Shortcut: press the target's letter (A, B...) to auto-aim and fire.

DEPENDENT INTERVENING CAUSE

It arises as a result of the first cause and is responsive to the defendant's conduct, e.g., the victim's own reaction or treatment by a doctor. The defendant stays liable unless the response was "abnormal" (not just unforeseeable, but unusual or extraordinary in hindsight).

INDEPENDENT INTERVENING CAUSE

Not foreseeable and not intended, e.g., the conduct or gross negligence of a third party, or a force of nature outside the defendant's control. It relieves the defendant when it is independent and unforeseeable.

QUESTIONS TO ASK (FROM THE SLIDES)

What was the nature of the intervening cause? Was it foreseeable? Who had control over it? How much harm did it contribute? Are there policy arguments, i.e., fairness and justice? Even with an intervening act, the defendant stays responsible unless that act was so out of the ordinary that holding the defendant responsible would be unjust.

BOSS: THE LEGAL CAUSE MOTHERSHIP

THE MISSION

The Mothership hovers at the top of the screen, launching four answer targets at a time. Shoot the right one to knock down its shields. Nine correct hits destroy it. This round is all proximate (legal) cause, including how causation works in felony murder. Correct hits are worth more, and a miss still costs a life.

PROXIMATE CAUSE

The prosecution must prove both actual and proximate causation. A death is foreseeable if it is the natural and probable result of the defendant's conduct. Even with an intervening act, the defendant stays responsible unless that act was so out of the ordinary that holding the defendant responsible would be unjust.

THE MPC VERSION

When the injury is generally of the type the defendant intended, proximate cause exists if the injury is not too remote or accidental in its occurrence to have a just bearing on the actor's liability.

FELONY MURDER: WHO CAUSED THE DEATH?

Majority rule: felon or agent. Minority rule: proximate cause. Under Redline, a felon generally isn't guilty of felony murder when a victim or officer kills a cofelon. The felony continues during flight; consider time, distance, and the causal relationship.

LIMITS ON CAUSATION

Common law: death has to occur within a year and a day of the injury. And providing a person the means to commit suicide generally doesn't make the provider guilty of murder, because suicide is not homicide.
